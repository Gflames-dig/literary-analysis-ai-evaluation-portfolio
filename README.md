## Sample 1 — Close Reading of an Original Passage

### Original demonstration passage

> The rain had stopped, but Mara kept the blue umbrella open as she stood beneath the station clock. Her brother had promised to return before the final train; now the platform was almost empty, and the clock's minute hand had begun to tremble in the wind.

### Interpretive question

**What might the open umbrella suggest about Mara's emotional state or situation?**

### Close-reading response

The open umbrella may suggest lingering expectation, a desire for protection, or difficulty adjusting to uncertainty. The strongest textual clue is the contrast between the stopped rain and Mara's decision to keep the umbrella open. The object remains in use after its practical purpose appears to have passed, which invites a symbolic reading. Her continued waiting for a promised return, together with the nearly empty platform and the approaching final train, adds pressure and uncertainty to the scene.

The interpretation should remain qualified. The passage does not explicitly state Mara's feelings, and it does not establish whether her brother will return. The umbrella could carry more than one meaning; a careful reading should explain plausible possibilities without treating one interpretation as proven fact.

### Evidence map

| Textual detail | Supported observation | Reasonable interpretation | What cannot be concluded from this passage alone |
|---|---|---|---|
| The rain has stopped, but the umbrella remains open. | The object is kept open after the rain ends. | It may symbolise lingering expectation, protection, or reluctance to change course. | That Mara is definitely afraid, grieving, or unwilling to go home. |
| Her brother promised to return before the final train. | Mara is waiting in response to a promise. | The scene involves expectation and a time limit. | That her brother has deliberately betrayed her. |
| The platform is almost empty and the clock hand trembles in the wind. | The setting feels exposed and time is passing. | These details build tension and uncertainty. | That a particular event will occur after the passage ends. |

## Sample 2 — Evaluating an AI-Generated Literary Interpretation

### Prompt

Interpret the possible symbolism of the open umbrella in the passage. Support your answer with details from the text and distinguish reasonable inference from certainty.

### Hypothetical AI response for review

> The umbrella proves that Mara is terrified of rain and shows that she hates her brother because he has abandoned her forever.

### Evaluation

**Overall judgment: Needs substantial revision.** The response identifies a potentially symbolic object but makes several claims that exceed the textual evidence.

| Issue | Evidence from the passage | Why it matters | Recommended correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overconfident wording: "proves" | The passage offers an object and a situation, not a direct statement of Mara's feelings. | A possible interpretation is presented as certainty. | Use calibrated language such as "may suggest" or "could symbolise." |
| Unsupported emotional claim: "terrified of rain" | The rain has already stopped; no fear is described. | The claim invents a motivation that the passage does not establish. | Ground the interpretation in the contrast between the stopped rain and the open umbrella. |
| Unsupported relationship claim: "hates her brother" | The text says she is waiting for him after a promise. | Waiting does not establish hatred or a specific emotional response. | State that the passage conveys expectation and uncertainty without specifying her exact feelings. |
| Unsupported finality: "abandoned her forever" | The passage ends while she is still waiting. | The response invents an outcome beyond the excerpt. | Acknowledge that the brother's eventual arrival or absence is unresolved. |

### Revised response

The open umbrella may symbolise lingering expectation or a desire for protection. Although the rain has stopped, Mara keeps the umbrella open while waiting for her brother, suggesting that she remains suspended in a situation whose outcome is uncertain. The nearly empty platform and the approaching final train reinforce the sense of time pressure. However, the passage does not establish that Mara is terrified, hates her brother, or has been abandoned permanently; those conclusions would require additional evidence.

### Rationale

The revision answers the question, cites relevant textual details, explains the interpretation, and marks the limits of what can be inferred. It improves precision without removing the passage's ambiguity.

## Sample 3 — Compact Literary-Analysis Evaluation Rubric

I would use the following dimensions when reviewing an AI-generated literary interpretation. The specific scoring anchors can be adapted to a project's instructions.

| Dimension | Strong response | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| Textual evidence | Cites relevant words, actions, images, or structural details. | Gives a conclusion without identifiable evidence. |
| Interpretive validity | Explains how evidence supports the reading. | Treats an association as proof or ignores contradictory details. |
| Calibration | Acknowledges ambiguity and uses confidence appropriate to the passage. | Presents a speculative interpretation as the only possible meaning. |
| Context and accuracy | Uses supplied context accurately and avoids invented facts about authors, works, or history. | Introduces unsupported biographical or historical claims. |
| Coherence | Provides a logical connection between observation and interpretation. | Contains contradictions or disconnected assertions. |
| Task adherence | Answers the actual question in the requested format and scope. | Summarises the plot instead of interpreting the requested feature. |

### Example scoring anchors

- **4 — Strong:** Accurate, evidence-led, logically explained, and appropriately calibrated.
- **3 — Mostly sound:** Useful interpretation with a minor omission or limited explanation.
- **2 — Material weaknesses:** Some relevant observations, but important claims are weakly supported or insufficiently explained.
- **1 — Poor:** Major misreading, invented details, contradictions, or failure to address the task.

Scores should be accompanied by specific examples, not just a number. When reviewers disagree, I would compare the disputed claim against the same passage and rubric, clarify the difference between evidence and inference, and document the reason for the final judgment.

## Sample 4 — Reproducible Error Record

**Error ID:** LIT-DEMO-001  
**Category:** Unsupported inference / overconfidence  
**Location:** Interpretation of the blue umbrella  
**Observed issue:** The response says the umbrella "proves" fear and that Mara's brother has abandoned her "forever."  
**Evidence check:** Neither the emotion nor the final outcome is stated. The excerpt instead establishes that the rain stopped and Mara continues waiting for a promised return.  
**Severity:** Material interpretive error, because the response changes an ambiguous scene into a certain claim about character motivation and plot outcome.  
**Recommended action:** Replace definitive claims with a text-supported interpretation; cite the contrast between the stopped rain and the open umbrella; identify what remains unknown.  
**Pass condition:** The revised response answers the question, uses evidence from the excerpt, avoids unsupported claims, and appropriately signals uncertainty.

## My Review Workflow

1. **Read for the task:** Identify the question, scope, genre, and any context supplied.
2. **Read the passage closely:** Mark significant wording, images, contrasts, repetitions, narrative choices, and structural details.
3. **Separate evidence from inference:** Identify what the text states, what it reasonably suggests, and what remains unknown.
4. **Test the interpretation:** Check whether each claim is supported, whether important details have been ignored, and whether other readings remain plausible.
5. **Evaluate the response:** Review accuracy, relevance, coherence, depth, calibration, and instruction adherence.
6. **Document the issue:** Record the claim, evidence, failure type, severity, recommended correction, and pass condition.
7. **Explain the judgment:** Provide concise, actionable reasoning so another reviewer can follow and reproduce the decision.
